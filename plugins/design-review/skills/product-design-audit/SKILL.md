# Product Design Audit

Audit the actual experience, not an imagined version of it.

## Evidence first

1. Identify the product surface and user task.
2. Inspect screenshots or capture the relevant states when tools permit.
3. Do not claim evidence for states that were not seen.
4. Do not claim full accessibility compliance from screenshots alone.

## Per-step review

For each important step or screen, assess:

- what is working visually
- whether the next action is visually obvious
- hierarchy and information density
- layout, spacing, typography, color, imagery, and component consistency
- friction, ambiguity, dead ends, or competing actions
- visible accessibility risks such as contrast, text size, focus treatment, or touch-target sizing
- loading, validation, empty, error, and confirmation states when available

Tie every material finding to the screen or step that supports it.

## Output

Return:

- overall verdict
- numbered step/screen review with health: strong / needs refinement / problematic
- highest-impact visual and product-design findings
- accessibility risks that are actually visible
- top recommended changes in priority order
- evidence limits: what could not be verified

Prefer plain language over broad design jargon. Never convert indirect research or memory into claimed audit evidence.
