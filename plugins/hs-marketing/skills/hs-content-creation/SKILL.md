# Hair Solutions Co. Content Creation

> **Merged from:** `content-creation` (Anthropic base) + `copywriting`, `content-research-writer`, `social-content` (Hair Solutions legacy)

Guidelines and frameworks for creating effective Hair Solutions Co. marketing content across channels — research, briefs, drafting, and pre-publish QA.

## Core Copywriting Principles

1. **Write to one person** — always address "you," never "customers"
2. **Lead with benefit, not feature** — outcomes > specifications
3. **Specificity sells** — "32% more hair growth in 4 weeks" > "visible results"
4. **One message per page** — don't split attention
5. **Match language to your reader** — use words they use, not industry jargon

## Content Research Process

### Step 1: Topic Intelligence

Before writing, gather:

- Primary keyword + 5–10 secondary/LSI keywords
- Top 3–5 ranking articles for the target keyword (analyze structure, not copy)
- Common questions (People Also Ask, Reddit, Quora, Answer The Public)
- Brand's unique angle or proprietary data point

### Step 2: Content Brief Template

```markdown
# Content Brief: [Article Title]

**Primary Keyword**: [keyword]
**Secondary Keywords**: [list]
**Target Audience**: [persona]
**Search Intent**: [Informational / Commercial / Transactional / Navigational]
**Content Goal**: [Drive bookings / Build authority / SEO traffic]
**Recommended Word Count**: [1,200–2,500 for guides; 500–800 for product pages]
**Key Points to Cover**: [list 5–8 points]
**Internal Links**: [2–4 pages to link to]
**CTA**: [What action should reader take?]
```

## Content Type Templates

### Blog Post Structure

1. **Headline** — clear, benefit-driven, includes primary keyword (under 60 characters)
2. **Introduction** (100–150 words) — hook with a relatable problem, question, surprising stat, or bold claim. Preview what the post covers. Include the primary keyword. Make an implicit promise of transformation/value.
3. **Body sections** (3–5 sections) — each with a descriptive H2; H3 for subsections. One core idea per section with supporting evidence, examples, or data.
4. **Conclusion** (100–150 words) — summarize key takeaways, restate the transformation/outcome, strong CTA.
5. **Meta description** — 150–160 characters, includes primary keyword, compels the click.

SEO-optimized article framework (canonical layout):

```
H1: [Primary Keyword + Hook — under 60 chars]

Introduction (100–150 words):
- Hook with relatable problem or surprising stat
- Preview what the article covers
- Implicit promise of transformation/value

H2: [Topic Section 1] (200–300 words)
H3: [Subtopic]
[Content + internal link opportunity]

H2: [Topic Section 2]
H3: [Subtopic with secondary keyword]
[Content]

H2: [Practical Tips / How-To Section]
[Numbered list or step-by-step]

H2: Common Mistakes / What to Avoid
[This earns featured snippets]

H2: FAQ (targets People Also Ask)
Q: [Question]
A: [2–3 sentence answer — concise for snippet]

Conclusion (100–150 words):
- Summarize key points
- Restate transformation/outcome
- Strong CTA
```

### Social Media Post Structure

- **Hook** — first line grabs attention (question, bold statement, number)
- **Body** — 2–4 concise points or a short narrative
- **CTA** — what should the reader do next (comment, click, share, tag)
- **Hashtags** — 3–5 relevant hashtags (platform-dependent; see Hashtag Strategy)

### Email Newsletter Structure — The 4-Part Newsletter

```
1. SUBJECT LINE (A/B test 2–3 variants):
   - Curiosity: "The hair mistake you're probably making"
   - Benefit: "3 ways to double your hair growth this month"
   - Urgency: "Last chance: 20% off ends Sunday"

2. PREVIEW TEXT: Extends the hook (40–90 chars) — complements the subject line, does not repeat it

3. BODY:
   - Opening hook (1–2 sentences)
   - Core value content (tips, story, update)
   - Transition to offer
   - CTA button (1 primary CTA only)

4. PS LINE: Best read section — put secondary CTA or key insight here
```

Also include a header/hero as visual anchor with a one-line value statement, and a footer with unsubscribe link, company info, and social links.

### Landing Page Structure

Above the fold (first impression):

```
[Headline]: One sentence — primary benefit + transformation
[Subheadline]: Expand on the promise, add specificity
[Hero Image/Video]: Show the result, not the process
[Primary CTA]: Action word + benefit ("Book My Transformation")
[Trust signal]: "500+ clients in [city]" / "4.9/5"
```

Body copy sections:

```
1. PROBLEM SECTION
   - Acknowledge the pain point in their language
   - Show you understand (empathy before expertise)

2. AGITATION
   - What's at stake if nothing changes?
   - Make the cost of inaction vivid

3. SOLUTION INTRODUCTION
   - Introduce your product/service as the bridge
   - Not "we do X" but "you get Y"

4. HOW IT WORKS
   - Simple 3-step process
   - Reduce perceived complexity

5. SOCIAL PROOF
   - Transformation testimonials (before/after)
   - Specific results, real names, photos if possible

6. OFFER DETAILS
   - What exactly is included?
   - Price framing (daily cost / per session)

7. OBJECTION HANDLING
   - "What if it doesn't work?" → Guarantee
   - "Is it for me?" → Who it's designed for
   - "What's the catch?" → Transparent answer

8. FINAL CTA
   - Restate the transformation
   - Urgency/scarcity if genuine
   - CTA button with benefit language
```

### Press Release Structure

- **Headline** — factual, newsworthy, under 80 characters
- **Subheadline** — optional, adds context
- **Dateline** — city, state, date
- **Lead paragraph** — who, what, when, where, why in 2–3 sentences
- **Body paragraphs** — supporting details, quotes, context
- **Boilerplate** — company description (standardized)
- **Media contact** — name, email, phone

### Case Study Structure

- **Title** — "[Customer] achieves [result] with [product]"
- **Snapshot** — customer name, industry, company size, product used, key result (sidebar or callout box)
- **Challenge** — what problem the customer faced
- **Solution** — what was implemented and how
- **Results** — quantified outcomes with specific metrics
- **Quote** — customer testimonial
- **CTA** — learn more, get a demo, read more case studies

## Writing Best Practices by Channel

### Blog & Long-Form Readability

- Sentences: average 15–20 words (max 30)
- Paragraphs: 2–4 sentences (never more than 5); one idea per paragraph
- Flesch-Kincaid target: 60–70 (Grade 7–8); adjust up for technical audiences
- Subheadings every 200–300 words
- Bullet points and numbered lists for 3+ items; bold key phrases (not entire sentences)
- Include a quote, data point, or example every 400–500 words
- Write in active voice; front-load key information in each section
- Start with "You" — address the reader directly
- Power words: transform, discover, proven, secret, instant, guaranteed

### Social Media Platform Strategies

**Instagram**

- **Feed posts**: transformation stories, product showcases, team spotlights (high-quality visuals)
- **Reels**: 15–60s tutorials, trending audio, behind-the-scenes — algorithm-prioritized
- **Stories**: daily touchpoints — polls, Q&As, countdowns, swipe-ups, flash offers
- **Carousels**: 5–10 slides for tutorials, tips, before/afters — highest save rate → reach boost
- Visual-first captions with storytelling hooks and line breaks; hashtags in first comment or at end
- **Optimal times**: Tue–Fri 11am–1pm and 7–9pm

**TikTok**

- Hook within first 1–3 seconds (state the problem or payoff immediately)
- Native vertical video, 21–34s optimal, up to 60s for tutorials
- Trending sounds boost discoverability; duets/stitches for engagement
- **Niche content over broad appeal** — the algorithm rewards deep niche engagement

**LinkedIn**

- Professional storytelling with a personal narrative arc — professional but human
- 3–5 sentence paragraphs, no walls of text; 1,300 characters is the sweet spot before "see more"
- Start with a hook line that creates curiosity or controversy
- End with a question to drive comments
- Articles and newsletters for authority building
- **Optimal times**: Tue–Thu 8–10am and 12pm

**Twitter/X**

- Threads for complex ideas (1 tweet = 1 point); concise, punchy, opinion-led
- Quote-tweet strategy to join trending conversations
- Short-form value bombs (tips, one-liners); engage with replies

**Facebook**

- Community groups for organic reach; conversational tone
- Long-form storytelling still works; questions drive comments
- Shorter posts (under 80 characters) get more engagement for links
- Facebook Live for events and Q&As; local targeting for service businesses

### Email

- Subject lines that create urgency, curiosity, or state clear value — under 50 characters
- Personalize where possible (name, company, behavior)
- One primary CTA per email — make it visually distinct
- Keep body copy scannable: bold key phrases, short paragraphs, bullet points
- Test everything: subject lines, send times, CTA copy, layout
- Mobile-first: most email is read on mobile

### Web (Landing Pages, Product Pages)

- Lead with benefits, not features
- Use "you" language — speak to the reader directly
- Minimize jargon unless the audience expects it
- Every section should answer "so what?" from the reader's perspective
- Reduce friction: fewer form fields, clear next steps, trust signals near CTAs

## SEO Fundamentals for Content

### Keyword Strategy

- Identify one primary keyword and 2–3 secondary keywords per piece
- Use the primary keyword in: headline, first paragraph, one subheading, meta description, URL slug
- Use secondary keywords naturally in body copy and subheadings
- Do not keyword-stuff — write for humans first

### On-Page SEO Checklist

- Title tag: under 60 characters, includes primary keyword
- Meta description: under 160 characters, includes primary keyword, compels click
- URL slug: short, descriptive, includes primary keyword
- H1: one per page, matches or closely reflects the title tag
- H2/H3: descriptive, include secondary keywords where natural
- Image alt text: descriptive, includes keyword where relevant
- Internal links: 2–3 links to related content on your site
- External links: 1–2 links to authoritative sources

### Content-SEO Integration

- Aim for comprehensive coverage of the topic (search engines reward depth)
- Answer related questions (check "People Also Ask" for ideas)
- Update and refresh high-performing content regularly
- Structure content for featured snippets: definition paragraphs, numbered lists, tables

### Pillar Content Strategy

Build topical authority with a hub-and-spoke model:

- **Pillar page**: comprehensive guide on a broad topic (3,000–5,000 words)
- **Cluster posts**: specific subtopics linking back to pillar (1,000–1,500 words each)
- **Internal linking**: all cluster posts link to pillar; pillar links to clusters

Example for Hair Solutions Co.:

```
Pillar: "Complete Guide to Hair Restoration Treatments"
Clusters:
- "Keratin Treatment vs. Brazilian Blowout"
- "How Long Does Hair Restoration Take?"
- "Best Hair Treatments for Damaged Hair"
- "Hair Restoration Treatment Costs"
```

## Headline and Hook Formulas

```

"From Thin & Damaged to Thick & Healthy Hair"

### Curiosity Headlines

```

What [Authority/Expert] Doesn't Tell You About [Topic]
"What Most Hair Salons Won't Tell You About Keratin Treatments"
```

### Direct Benefit Headlines

```
Get [Specific Result] in [Timeframe]
"Get Noticeably Fuller Hair in 30 Days"

[Number] Ways to [Achieve Goal]
"7 Ways to Stop Hair Loss Before It Gets Worse"
```

### General Headline Formulas

- **How to [achieve result] [without common obstacle]** — "How to Double Your Email Open Rates Without Sending More Emails"
- **[Number] [adjective] ways to [achieve result]** — "7 Proven Ways to Reduce Customer Churn"
- **Why [common belief] is wrong (and what to do instead)** — "Why More Content Is Not the Answer (And What to Do Instead)"
- **The [adjective] guide to [topic]** — "The Complete Guide to B2B Content Marketing"
- **[Do this], not [that]** — "Build a Community, Not Just an Audience"
- **What [impressive result] taught us about [topic]** — "What 10,000 A/B Tests Taught Us About Email Subject Lines"
- **[topic]: what [audience] needs to know in [year]** — "SEO: What Marketers Need to Know in 2025"

### Hook Formulas (Opening Lines)

- **Surprising statistic**: "73% of marketers say their biggest challenge is not budget — it is focus."
- **Contrarian statement**: "The best marketing campaigns start with saying no to most channels."
- **Question**: "When was the last time a marketing email actually changed what you bought?"
- **Scenario**: "Imagine launching a campaign and knowing, before it goes live, which messages will land."
- **Bold claim**: "Most landing pages lose half their visitors in the first three seconds."
- **Story opening**: "Last quarter, our team was spending 20 hours a week on reporting. Here is what we did about it."

### Monthly Planning

- Week 1: Awareness content (attract new audience)
- Week 2: Education content (build trust)
- Week 3: Conversion content (drive bookings/sales)
- Week 4: Community content (build loyalty)

### Content Batching Workflow

1. **Theme week in blocks** — 2 hours per week
2. Write all captions first, then match to visuals
3. Use Notion or Google Sheets for the content calendar
4. Pre-schedule 2 weeks ahead using Later/Buffer/Meta Suite
5. Leave 20% unscheduled for reactive/trending content

### Viral Content Framework (6 Steps)

1. **Find** — identify top-performing content in your niche using native search + SparkAds
2. **Analyze** — hook, structure, format, emotional trigger
3. **Extract** — what principle made it work?
4. **Adapt** — apply the principle to your brand/niche
5. **Enhance** — add your POV, data, or personal story
6. **Post + Remix** — publish, then create a follow-up (duet, reply, thread)

### Hashtag Strategy

- 3–5 niche hashtags (10k–500k posts) — high discoverability
- 1–2 broad hashtags (brand awareness)
- 1–2 location hashtags (local service businesses)
- Avoid banned hashtags — check via Instagram search

### Engagement Strategy

- Reply to every comment within 60 minutes of posting
- Pin the best comment to boost algorithm signals
- DM new followers with a genuine (non-spammy) welcome
- Engage with 5–10 accounts in your niche before posting

## Call-to-Action Best Practices

### CTA Principles

- Use action verbs: "Get", "Start", "Download", "Join", "Try", "See"
- Be specific about what happens next: "Start your free trial" beats "Submit"
- Create urgency only when genuine
- Reduce risk: "No credit card required", "Cancel anytime", "Free for 14 days"
- One primary CTA per page or email — too many choices reduce conversions

### CTA Copy — Instead of "Submit" or "Buy Now"

```
"Start My Transformation"
"Book My Free Consultation"
"Get My [Specific Result]"
"Reserve My Spot"
"See How It Works"
"Claim My [Discount/Free Item]"
```

### Micro-copy Under Buttons (Reduces Friction)

```
"No credit card required"
"Cancel anytime"
"Free 15-min call — no commitment"
"Join [N] clients who already transformed their hair"
```

### CTA Examples by Context

- **Blog post**: "Read our complete guide to [topic]" / "Subscribe for weekly insights"
- **Landing page**: "Start free trial" / "Get a demo" / "See pricing"
- **Email**: "Read the full story" / "Claim your spot" / "Reply and tell us"
- **Social media**: "Drop a comment if you agree" / "Save this for later" / "Link in bio"
- **Case study**: "See how [product] can work for your team" / "Talk to our team"

### CTA Placement

- Above the fold on landing pages (do not make users scroll to act)
- After establishing value in emails (not in the first sentence)
- At the end of blog posts (after you have earned the reader's trust)
- In-line within content when contextually relevant
- Repeat the primary CTA at the bottom of long-form pages

```

## Price Framing Techniques

- **Daily cost**: "$199 treatment = $6.60/day for 30 days of transformed hair"
- **Cost of inaction**: "Trying 5 different shampoos at $30 each = $150. Our treatment = $199. Once."
- **Anchoring**: show the higher-priced option first, then mid-tier (which you want to sell)
- **Value stacking**: list everything included before revealing price

## Pre-Publish Quality Checklist

- [ ] Primary keyword in H1, first 100 words, and meta description
- [ ] Meta description 150–160 characters with CTA
- [ ] All images have descriptive alt text
- [ ] At least 2 internal links + 1–2 authoritative external links
- [ ] Read aloud test — does it sound natural?

- [ ] CTA is clear and above the fold or in first scroll
