# Higgsfield Soul Cast

Read `../../references/production-contract.md`. Use Soul Cast for designed actors; use `../higgsfield-soul-id` when the character must be a particular real person.

## Write the casting brief first

Define the actor's story function before appearance:

- genre and world;
- narrative archetype;
- era;
- perceived production polish;
- identity and audience relationship;
- age, build, height, eyes, hair, facial hair, and distinctive details;
- wardrobe and continuity rules;
- prohibited stereotypes or visual shortcuts.

Soul Cast exposes genre, era, archetype, identity, physical appearance, details, and outfit as independent casting controls. Use them deliberately instead of packing every decision into free text.

## Build the cast

1. Generate a small casting board with one meaningful variable between candidates.
2. Select the actor who best serves the role, not simply the most attractive face.
3. Validate neutral close-up, three-quarter, full-height, and emotion frames.
4. Record the chosen parameter set and protected traits.
5. Promote the actor into the Cinema Studio project and test one low-complexity shot before action, dialogue, or multi-character scenes.

## Best uses

- recurring fictional narrators and presenters;
- film and episodic casts;
- era- and genre-specific music-video actors;
- campaign personas designed for a particular audience;
- previsualization before committing to a cast direction.

For localization, change representation intentionally and review each actor as a distinct creative choice. Do not treat race, age, body type, or cultural presentation as a mechanical skin swap.

## Review

Reject waxy skin, generic symmetry, unstable age, hairstyle drift, wardrobe changes, stereotyped representation, or actors who do not hold their archetype across expressions. Keep a cast bible with approved frames and role-specific continuity notes.
